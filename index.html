The Great War

From Urus Encyclopedia, the free encyclopedia

The Great War, also known as the War of the Gods, was a global conflict that lasted thirteen generations, beginning approximately at the end of Urus' 47th wval A traditional unit of time equivalent to approximately 10,000 generations, or 200,000 years. , and resulted in unprecedented destruction throughout the known world.

The conflict fundamentally altered the political, geographical, and magical landscape of modern Urus.

Casualties

Estimates regarding the number of deaths resulting from the Great War vary considerably. Contemporary records have proven difficult to recover, and historians generally consider it highly likely that a significant portion of these records was destroyed during the conflict.

As such, the exact number of casualties remains unknown.

It is estimated that approximately 60% of the human population was lost during the war, along with approximately 80% of the godly population .

The siren population also suffered severe losses, although reliable estimates remain difficult to establish. [3]

Among other magical and non-magical creatures, at least 42 species were rendered extinct , while a further 23 species experienced severe population decline. [4]

Geographical impact

The Great War resulted in permanent geographical changes to several regions of Urus.

Approximately 40% of the world's landmass is estimated to have been submerged, destroyed, or otherwise rendered uninhabitable during the conflict.

Some of the territories lost during the war eventually became what are now known as the Underwater Kingdoms .

The full extent of the geographical changes remains difficult to determine, as maps and geographical records from the period preceding the war are incomplete.

Participants and sides

The sides involved in the Great War remain a subject of historical debate.

For centuries, the conflict was commonly understood to have been a war between divine beings and humans. However, surviving sources suggest that the divine beings may have been targeting one another from the earliest stages of the conflict, with humans and other living beings becoming involved primarily as a consequence of the ensuing destruction.

This interpretation is currently supported by the largest body of surviving historical evidence and is considered by many historians to be the most plausible explanation for the conflict's alternative name, the War of the Gods.

Nevertheless, the precise nature of the alliances and opposing factions remains uncertain. No complete record of the political or military structure of the conflict has survived.

Origins

The precise circumstances that led to the Great War remain the subject of considerable historical debate. No single explanation has been universally accepted, and the surviving evidence is insufficient to conclusively establish the events that initiated the conflict.

Divine Corruption

The Divine Corruption is currently regarded as the most promising theory concerning the origins of the Great War. [7]

According to this hypothesis, an unidentified phenomenon affected one or more divine beings prior to the beginning of the conflict, ultimately contributing to the hostilities between them.

The nature, origin, and extent of the alleged corruption remain unknown. While several surviving sources have been interpreted as evidence supporting the theory, none have been considered conclusive.

Other theories

Several alternative theories regarding the origins of the Great War remain under consideration. These include explanations involving human intervention, internal conflict among divine beings, magical catastrophes, and other unidentified events.

Although many of these theories have been dismissed by mainstream historians, none can be conclusively ruled out due to the scarcity and incomplete nature of surviving evidence.

Notes

  1. wval: A traditional unit of historical time equivalent to approximately 10,000 generations, or 200,000 years. The exact duration of a generation varies according to species and historical period, making conversions approximate.
  2. Godly population: The estimated figure refers to the total known divine population preceding the Great War.
  3. Siren casualties: Reliable estimates of total siren casualties cannot be established. Sirens are divided into two distinct subspecies that experienced substantially different mortality rates during the war. Approximately 50% of one subspecies is estimated to have perished, while mortality among the other is believed to have exceeded 90%.
  4. Species affected: See Species affected by the Great War for a complete list.
  5. Geographical changes: Estimates vary depending on the definition of "landmass" and whether territories rendered permanently uninhabitable are included.
  6. Underwater Kingdoms: Several territories submerged during the Great War are now inhabited and governed by aquatic populations.
  7. Divine Corruption: The term refers specifically to the historical hypothesis concerning the origins of the Great War and should not be confused with later uses of the term in magical or theological contexts.
